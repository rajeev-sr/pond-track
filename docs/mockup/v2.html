The aim

Where should this
village build its pond?

A Gram Panchayat has a contour sheet and a budget. Contour answers the question that sheet cannot: which point on this land will hold water, how much catchment feeds it, how much it will yield in a normal year, and how big the structure should be.

InputContour survey · KML / KMZ
OutputRanked sites · catchment · yield · design
ScaleVillage, 1–10 km²
01
Problem

Four ways a village pond is put in the wrong place.

Siting is usually settled by eye, or by an engineer's single visit. The contour sheet that already exists for the village holds the answer, but reading catchment area and storage off it by hand is slow and rarely done. Each failure below wastes public money in a different way.

1.1

Too little catchment

The structure is built, and never fills. Contributing area was never measured — only guessed from the look of the ground.

1.2

Water already there

The chosen spot is an existing tank or a river bed. It is the wettest ground on the sheet, which is exactly why a terrain-only judgement points at it.

1.3

Storage that cannot be built

A capacity is promised that the ground will not give without excavation deeper than the budget, or a bund longer than the land available.

1.4

No record of the reasoning

When the pond underperforms, nothing on file says which assumptions were made, so the next one repeats the mistake.

02
Approach

From contour lines to a dimensioned proposal.

Four stages. Each one leaves an artefact you can inspect and disagree with — a surface, a flow network, a ranking, a design — rather than a single number to take on trust.

Stage 01

Surface

Contour polylines are triangulated into a metric elevation grid, in the UTM zone the sheet itself falls in.

Stage 02

Flow

Depressions conditioned, flow directions assigned, accumulation traced — giving channels, catchments and time of concentration.

Stage 03

Site

Nine weighted criteria rank the buildable ground that survives the exclusions — water, rivers, roads, buildings.

Stage 04

Design

Stage–storage from the surface, yield from rainfall and soil, then depth, footprint, cost and the constraint that binds.

Method

How the proposal is arrived at

Six stages, each a published method. The intermediate artefact of every stage is available through the API, so a reviewer can check the surface, the flow network or the ranking independently rather than accepting the final figure.

1 · Surface

Contour polylines carry elevation; the ground between them does not exist in the file. Vertices are triangulated and the surface interpolated linearly within each triangle, giving a regular grid whose cell size follows contour spacing — 5 m on the sample sheet.

The working coordinate system is derived from the sheet's own longitude, so slope, area and distance are computed in metres. Nothing about the location is fixed in the code: the sample resolves to UTM 44N, and test sheets in four other zones — including the southern hemisphere — resolve correctly.

2 · Flow

Closed depressions are raised to their spill level by Priority-Flood with an ε gradient, so water still has a descending path across filled ground. Where a depression is an artefact of a road or bund crossing a channel, a bounded least-cost breach is cut instead of filling.

Flow direction follows the eight-neighbour steepest descent. Accumulation is traced in dependency order so every cell is visited once. A cell becomes a channel above a contributing-area threshold — 1 ha by default, deliberately small, because a nala draining a few hectares is precisely where a check dam belongs.

Tc = 0.01947 · L0.77 · S−0.385 Time of concentration, Kirpich (1940). L is the longest flow path in metres, S its average gradient.

Barnes, Lehman & Mulla (2014) · Lindsay (2016) · Strahler (1957)

3 · Exclusions

Some ground cannot hold a pond however well it scores, and the veto is applied to the buildable mask before ranking, so excluded ground is never proposed and then withdrawn. Each standoff follows from what the hazard does to a structure.

FeatureStandoffReason
Existing tank0 mIts bank is sound ground; only the water is barred.
River, canal50 mA village pond cannot impound a river, and the land beside one floods.
Building50 mImpoundment against a dwelling is a safety matter.
Road20 mThe embankment and its borrow area need the room.
Stream, drain, ditchnoneNever excluded — a check dam belongs on one.

A river is read from both conventions used to map it: the centreline and the areal water body, which carries no waterway tag at all. Reading only the first classified a 563.6 ha river as standing water and left 64.7 ha of its bank open to proposals.

4 · Ranking

Nine criteria, weighted by the Analytic Hierarchy Process. Weights are the principal eigenvector of the pairwise comparison matrix, and the matrix is tested for consistency before any weight is used.

5 · Yield

Runoff follows SCS-CN with the initial abstraction taken at 0.3S rather than the textbook 0.2S, following Central Water Commission and IMD practice for Indian catchments. The curve number comes from hydrologic soil group and land cover, adjusted for antecedent moisture.

Q = (P − 0.3S)² / (P + 0.7S)   S = 25400/CN − 254 P and Q in mm. Q is zero for P ≤ 0.3S.

A single empirical formula should not be trusted alone, so the result is cross-checked against four independent Indian relations — Inglis–DeSouza (1929), Khosla (1960), Barlow (1912) and the Rational method. Design yield is the 75 % dependable value from the annual series, which is the basis used for minor irrigation works.

6 · Design

The stage–storage relation is built by flood-filling the conditioned surface level by level. Volume between two levels uses the prismoidal rule, exact for a linearly varying cross-section:

V = (d/3) · ( Atop + Abottom + √(Atop · Abottom) ) d is the level increment; A the plan area impounded at each level.

Design depth is set by whichever constraint binds first — practical excavation depth, available land, sustainable share of catchment yield, or side-slope closure — and the report names it. A depth quoted without its binding constraint invites the wrong next question.

A monthly balance then subtracts open-water evaporation and seepage by soil group from inflow, over a three-year spin-up, and reports how many months the pond holds water above dead storage.

7 · Limitations

  • The surface is interpolated, not surveyed. A hollow smaller than the contour interval cannot appear in it at all.
  • Flow is routed to a single neighbour. On genuinely flat ground this gives parallel flow lines where real water spreads.
  • A catchment clipped by the sheet edge is a lower bound, and is reported as such rather than as a measurement.
  • Yield is a design estimate. SCS-CN is an accepted method, not a gauge reading; the cross-checks bound it, they do not calibrate it.
  • Tenure is outside the model. Ownership must be confirmed from the cadastral record.
